# How a bonus is taxed

<!-- https://www.irs.gov/publications/p15 | Finnie note, 2026-11-25 -->

A bonus from your employer is **regular wages** for tax purposes. It is added to your other pay for the year and taxed at your normal income-tax rates, and it also owes Social Security and Medicare tax like any paycheck. There is no special "bonus tax rate"; what confuses people is how much is **withheld** from it.

## Withholding vs the tax you actually owe

Employers often treat bonuses as **supplemental wages** and withhold federal income tax at a flat **22%** (or **37%** on supplemental wages above $1 million in a year), instead of using your normal W-4 withholding. Some employers instead add the bonus to a regular paycheck and withhold as if you earned that much every pay period, which can look like a very high rate.

Withholding is only a prepayment. Your actual tax is worked out on your return from your total income for the year:

- if your top bracket is **12%**, 22% withholding on a bonus is more than the tax on it, and the extra comes back as part of your refund;
- if your top bracket is **24% or higher**, 22% is less than the tax on it, and you may owe a little more when you file.

## Social Security and Medicare

The bonus also has 6.2% Social Security tax withheld (until your total wages for the year reach the Social Security wage base, $184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% Medicare tax, plus 0.9% additional Medicare tax on wages above $200,000.

## Lowering the tax on a bonus

- Contributing part of a bonus to a traditional 401(k), if your plan allows it, lowers the income tax on it (not the Social Security and Medicare tax).
- A bonus doesn't push your **other** income into a higher bracket; only the part of income above each bracket line is taxed at the higher rate.

## Example

A single employee earning $70,000 gets a $10,000 bonus. Her employer withholds 22% federal income tax ($2,200) plus 7.65% for Social Security and Medicare ($765). Her total wages of $80,000 keep her top bracket at 22%, so the tax on the bonus is about $2,200 and the withholding is close to right.
