# Student loan interest deduction

<!-- https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc456 | Finnie note, 2026-11-25 -->

If you pay interest on a qualified student loan, you can deduct up to **$2,500** of that interest a year. It is an "above-the-line" deduction: it lowers your adjusted gross income, so you get it **whether or not you itemize**.

## Who qualifies

- The loan was taken out to pay qualified higher-education costs (tuition, fees, room and board, books, supplies) for you, your spouse, or someone who was your dependent when you took the loan.
- You are legally obligated to pay the loan and you actually paid the interest during the year.
- Your filing status is **not** married filing separately, and no one can claim you as a dependent.
- Loans from a relative or from an employer plan don't qualify.

Voluntary interest payments count, including interest paid during a deferment or grace period, as long as you make them.

## The income limit

The deduction is reduced at higher incomes and disappears entirely above a limit. For 2026 it phases out for single filers with modified adjusted gross income between **$85,000 and $100,000**; for married couples filing jointly the range is roughly twice as high. IRS Publication 970 has the exact figures for each year, which are adjusted for inflation.

## How much it saves

The deduction lowers your taxable income, so the saving is the deduction times your tax bracket: $2,500 of interest saves $300 in the 12% bracket and $550 in the 22% bracket. Your loan servicer sends **Form 1098-E** if you paid $600 or more of interest.

## Related: student loan forgiveness

Student loan debt that is forgiven or cancelled is generally taxable income, with exceptions: forgiveness because of death or permanent disability, Public Service Loan Forgiveness, and certain teacher and other public-service programs are not taxed. Forgiveness under income-driven repayment plans was tax-free federally through 2025 but becomes taxable again from 2026 under current law.

## Example

A single teacher earning $60,000 paid $1,800 of student loan interest. She deducts all $1,800 (she's under the income limit), lowering her taxable income by $1,800. In the 12% bracket that saves $216.
