# Estimated quarterly tax

<!-- https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/estimated-taxes | Finnie note, 2026-11-25 -->

The US tax system is pay-as-you-go: tax is due during the year as you earn the income, not only in April. For a job, your employer does this for you by withholding tax from each paycheck (based on your Form W-4). Income with no withholding, such as freelance or self-employment earnings, rental income, interest, dividends, capital gains, or a large one-off sale, is covered instead by **estimated tax payments** made four times a year with Form 1040-ES.

## Who has to pay estimated tax

You generally need to make estimated payments if you expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when you file, after subtracting your withholding and refundable credits. Freelancers and small-business owners usually must, because nothing is withheld from their earnings, and their payments also cover self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare for the self-employed). Someone whose only income is a salary with enough withholding usually does not.

## Due dates

The four payments for a tax year are due around **April 15, June 15, September 15, and January 15 of the next year**. When a date falls on a weekend or holiday, it moves to the next business day. The "quarters" are uneven: the June payment covers only two months of income.

## Avoiding the underpayment penalty (safe harbor)

If you pay too little during the year, the IRS charges a penalty that works like interest on the amount that was late. You avoid it if your withholding plus estimated payments, paid on time, add up to at least the smaller of:

- **90% of this year's tax**, or
- **100% of last year's tax** (**110%** if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000, or $75,000 if married filing separately).

The "last year" rule is popular because it is a known number: if you pay one quarter of last year's tax each quarter, you owe no penalty even if this year's income jumps, though you still owe the rest of the tax by the April filing deadline.

## Ways to pay

You can pay online through IRS Direct Pay or the Electronic Federal Tax Payment System, by debit or credit card, or by check with a 1040-ES voucher. A worker with a side job can often skip estimated payments by asking their employer to withhold more on Form W-4, because withholding counts as if it were paid evenly through the year.

## Example

A graphic designer earns $40,000 freelancing and expects about $8,000 of income tax and self-employment tax. Nothing is withheld, so she pays about $2,000 on each of the four due dates. If last year's tax was $6,000, paying $1,500 a quarter (100% of last year's tax) would already protect her from the penalty, with the remaining $2,000 due when she files.
