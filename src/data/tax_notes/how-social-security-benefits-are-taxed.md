# How Social Security benefits are taxed

<!-- https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc423 | Finnie note, 2026-11-25 -->

Social Security income (retirement, survivor, and disability benefits) can be partly taxable as income, depending on your other income. **At most 85% of your benefits is ever taxable**; at lower incomes none of it is. (Supplemental Security Income, SSI, is never taxable.)

## Provisional income

The test uses a figure often called **provisional income** (or "combined income"):

provisional income = your adjusted gross income without the benefits + tax-exempt interest (such as municipal bond interest) + **half of your Social Security benefits**

## The thresholds

| Filing status | None taxable | Up to 50% taxable | Up to 85% taxable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single, head of household | provisional income up to $25,000 | $25,000 – $34,000 | above $34,000 |
| Married filing jointly | up to $32,000 | $32,000 – $44,000 | above $44,000 |
| Married filing separately and living together | — | — | from the first dollar |

These thresholds are written into the law and **are not adjusted for inflation**, so more retirees owe tax on their benefits each year as their other income rises.

"Up to 50%" and "up to 85%" are caps, not flat rates: the taxable amount rises gradually as provisional income passes the thresholds. The taxable part of your benefits is then taxed at your normal income-tax bracket. Your Form SSA-1099 shows the benefits you received; the IRS worksheet in the Form 1040 instructions works out the taxable amount.

## Ways people manage it

- Withdrawals from a traditional IRA or 401(k), pensions, wages, and capital gains all raise provisional income; Roth IRA withdrawals do not.
- You can ask the Social Security Administration to withhold federal tax from benefits (Form W-4V, at 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22%) instead of making estimated payments.
- Taxpayers 65 and older also get the extra standard deduction and, for 2025-2028, the $6,000 senior deduction, which lower the tax on taxable benefits. Some states tax benefits too; most do not.

## Example

A married couple filing jointly receives $40,000 of Social Security and has $30,000 of pension income. Provisional income is $30,000 + $20,000 (half the benefits) = $50,000, which is over $44,000, so part of their benefits falls in the 85% tier. Working through the IRS worksheet, $11,100 of their $40,000 of benefits is taxable.
