# News index: token statistics and retrieval test

Measurements of real Yahoo Finance news stories and a retrieval test of two ways to index them, using the same metrics as the knowledge-base reports ([kb-token-stats-basics.md](kb-token-stats-basics.md)). Design: [news-index.md](news-index.md). Generated by `python -m scripts.eval_news`; only the findings are written by hand.

- **Sample:** 190 unique stories from the news cache of the MARKET, AAPL, NVDA, AMD, SPY, SPCX and AM feeds, captured 2026-10-01T19:13:19Z, frozen in [news_snapshot.json](../tests/data/news_snapshot.json). Titles and summaries are Yahoo's own text.
- **Tokenizer:** `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` (the embedding model), without `[CLS]`/`[SEP]`. Its input limit is 512 tokens.
- **Questions:** 51 in [news_eval.yaml](../tests/data/news_eval.yaml): 40 answerable by one or more stories, 6 out of scope (not finance) and 5 near-miss (finance, but the snapshot has no story on it).
- **Variants:** *title+summary* is what the index stores (`"{title}\n{summary}"`); *title only* indexes and returns the headline alone.
- **Search:** top 5, no ticker or date filter (the snapshot is a frozen sample, so the date window is open). Raw results: [news_eval_results.csv](data/news_eval_results.csv), [news_eval_summary.csv](data/news_eval_summary.csv).

## Reproduce

```bash
python -m scripts.eval_news
```

## Retrieval test: title+summary vs title only

## Generation test: answer quality

<!-- generation:start -->
`python -m scripts.eval_news_generation`: for each question and variant, the top-5 stories go to `gpt-4o-mini` (the app's model, temperature 0) with the instruction to answer only from them (<= 150 words) or say they don't cover the question. The same model then judges the answer against the stories and the question's reference answer and key points ([news_eval_reference.yaml](../tests/data/news_eval_reference.yaml)). The prompts, grade schema and refusal detector are the knowledge-base test's (`scripts/eval_generation.py`), so the scores are comparable. Because the same model writes and judges, scores may be lenient.

| Metric | Scale | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 1-5 | Does the answer address the question asked? |
| Faithfulness | 0-1 | Share of the answer's claims supported by the retrieved stories (hallucination check); not scored for refusals |
| Correctness | 1-5 | Do the answer's facts agree with the reference answer? |
| Completeness | 0-1 | Share of the reference key points covered; 0 for a refused answerable question |

n32 and n33 are retrieval-only: their stories never name the winner, so a correct "the summary doesn't say" would be scored as a refusal. Raw results with every answer, unsupported claim and judge reason: [news_eval_generation.csv](data/news_eval_generation.csv), [news_eval_generation_summary.csv](data/news_eval_generation_summary.csv).

## Findings

<!-- findings:start -->
- **Read the 100% with care.** The 40 answerable questions were written by reading these same 190 stories, so most reuse a story's own
  words ("Why did Oura postpone its IPO?" against a story titled about Oura's IPO freeze). Real users will paraphrase more, and the live
  index will hold many more stories than 190. The numbers show the method is sound and the summary helps; they are not a hit rate to
  expect in production. The KB question set (74% hit@1) was harder on purpose.
- **The summary earns its tokens, for the answer text.** Title only still finds the right story (hit@1 38 of 40, hit@5 39), because
  headlines are specific. What it loses is the answer: hint@5 falls from 40 to 27 of 40, since the figures and reasons are in the
  summary ("$100 billion in buyback" is not in the headline of n08, which the title-only index misses entirely). Title+summary sends
  about 320 tokens for the top 5 against about 88, which is small next to the knowledge base's 800-1,300.
- **Stories are small enough to need no chunking.** Title+summary is 18-165 tokens (median 62) against the 512 limit, so one point per story
  is right; there is nothing to split, unlike the KB sections.
- **Score gap:** answerable top-1 scores are 0.70-0.93 (median 0.80), out-of-scope at most 0.556, near-miss at most 0.64. On this set any
  `min_score` from 0.65 to 0.70 separates all 51 questions; the current 0.60 passes every near-miss (0 of 5 return nothing, e.g. "chip
  export restrictions" returns an AMD deal story at 0.63). Full-sentence questions score higher than keyword queries: earlier hand
  tests ("Apple iPhone" 0.67, "Federal Reserve interest rates" 0.68, "Bitcoin and crypto" 0.68) still clear 0.65. A provisional 0.65 is
  supported by both; it has not been applied.
- **The filter keeps 1.7 stories per answerable question, 82% of them correct,** so the answer is not padded with unrelated stories.
- **Not measured:** the answer-quality test (relevance, faithfulness, correctness, completeness) needs an LLM judge, which this
  environment couldn't reach. Ticker and date filters are tested in the unit tests, not here. Retention and the live poll are not retrieval.
- **Answer quality (title+summary) is close to the knowledge base, a little lower:** relevance 4.76, faithfulness 0.96, correctness 4.79,
  completeness 0.90 against 4.91 / 0.98 / 4.90 / 0.96 for the 93 KB questions (a different question set). The gap is mostly completeness:
  headline-level sources don't hold every key point (n30 covers the cause of the bond losses but not that stocks held up and crypto rose;
  n17 omits Citi's Buy rating).
- **Title only can't answer.** It refuses 22 of 38 answerable questions because the headline rarely contains the figure or reason
  (relevance 2.68, completeness 0.35). Its faithfulness of 0.85 is over the 16 answers it gave, so it isn't a strength. Together with the
  retrieval result this settles it: keep embedding and returning the summary.
- **Unanswerable questions are all declined (11 of 11, both variants), including the 5 near-misses** that retrieval still answered with
  related stories at scores of 0.60-0.64. So the answering model is the safety net for near-misses today. A `min_score` of 0.65 would stop
  them before any model call (see the score gap above).
- **Where title+summary lost points:** n34 was wrongly refused although its story says the biotech ETF barely reacted to the tariff. n06
  said "the sources do not specify the five stocks" although the headline names Nvidia and Intel; the shared refusal detector doesn't match
  "specify", so it counted as an answer (relevance 1, faithfulness 0), which pulls the faithfulness mean down. n27 called Trump "former
  President", which no story says.
- **The judge was lenient at least once.** n16 added "on October 1, 2026" (the story says only "Monday") and the judge still scored it fully
  faithful. The writer and judge are the same model, so treat 0.96 as an upper bound.
- **Not tested here:** the app's own answer step (the synthesizer prompt, which differs from this test's), the 150-word limit's effect on
  completeness, ticker and date filters, and multi-turn questions. "Answers with every claim supported" excludes refusals, so it is low for
  title only by construction.
