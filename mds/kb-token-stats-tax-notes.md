# Knowledge-base token statistics: tax-notes

Measurements of the `tax-notes` source pages, a retrieval test of two chunk limits and an answer-quality test of the same
limits. The design these numbers feed into is [rag-data-design.md](rag-data-design.md); all categories side by side:
[kb-eval-summary.md](kb-eval-summary.md). Generated by `python -m scripts.kb_report --category tax-notes` from the
CSVs in [data/](data/); only the notes and findings are written by hand.

<!-- notes:start -->
**Added 2026-11-26, not yet in the measurements below:** a tenth note, home office and work expenses for employees (W-2 employees can't deduct them; the self-employed can), 4 chunks, and an eleventh, the wash sale rule, 5 chunks; both upserted into `finnie_kb` without a rebuild.

**First measurement (2026-11-25):** nine notes written in our own words (spec FR-K7) for personal-tax topics that the
Wikipedia pages cover badly or not at all: estimated quarterly tax, selling a home, how dividends and interest are taxed,
Social Security benefits, cryptocurrency, the student loan interest deduction, bonuses, rental income, RSUs and stock
options. Each is 300–600 words, written from the IRS publication or topic page in its header (its `source_url`, which the
answers link to), with figures checked against IRS sources on 2026-11-25. They live in `src/data/tax_notes/` (committed;
`rag.local_categories` tells build_index to read them instead of fetching from Wikipedia), are labelled "Finnie note, from
the IRS" in answers, and are searched first by the tax agent. Questions q01–q18 (two per note) and two out of scope.
<!-- notes:end -->

- **Sample:** the 9 `tax-notes` pages in [kb_topics.yaml](../src/data/kb_topics.yaml), full Wikipedia text (not
  truncated), saved in [tests/data/kb_snapshot/tax-notes/](../tests/data/kb_snapshot/tax-notes/).
- **Tokenizer:** `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`, the embedding model, without `[CLS]`/`[SEP]`.
- **Raw vs clean:** "raw" is the text as `WikiClient.get_page()` returns it. "Clean" has each MathML formula dump collapsed
  to inline LaTeX; all section and paragraph figures use clean text.
- **Section** = text under one heading (`##`–`#####`), excluding its subsections. **Paragraph** = one line of the source
  text; list items count as separate paragraphs.

Data: [tax-notes_docs.csv](data/tax-notes_docs.csv), [tax-notes_sections.csv](data/tax-notes_sections.csv), [tax-notes_paragraphs.csv](data/tax-notes_paragraphs.csv).

## Generation test: answer quality

`python -m scripts.eval_generation --category tax-notes`: for each question and limit, the top-5 chunks go to `gpt-4o-mini`
(the app's model, temperature 0) with the instruction to answer only from them (≤ 150 words) or say they don't cover
the question. `gpt-4o-mini` then judges the answer against the chunks and the question's `reference` / `key_points`.
Because the same model writes and judges, scores may be lenient.

| Metric | Scale | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | 1–5 | Does the answer address the question asked? |
| Faithfulness | 0–1 | Share of the answer's claims supported by the retrieved chunks (hallucination check); not scored for refusals |
| Correctness | 1–5 | Do the answer's facts agree with the reference answer? |
| Completeness | 0–1 | Share of the reference key points covered; 0 for a refused answerable question |

Refusals ("the sources don't cover this") are detected from the answer text. Raw results, with every answer, unsupported
claim and judge reason: [eval_tax-notes_generation.csv](data/eval_tax-notes_generation.csv), [eval_tax-notes_generation_summary.csv](data/eval_tax-notes_generation_summary.csv).

## Findings

<!-- findings:start -->
- All 18 answerable questions find an expected section in the top 5 (15 first, the other three a neighbouring section of the same note); answers are relevant (5.00), correct (4.89) and faithful
  (0.99). Both out-of-scope questions were declined.
- Searched together with the 353 Wikipedia tax and retirement pages (the tax agent's search), the ten everyday questions
  that previously landed on loosely related pages (estimated tax, selling a home, dividends, savings interest, Social
  Security, crypto, student loan interest, bonuses, rental income, RSUs) now all find the matching note first (scores
  0.78–0.87). "How is Social Security income taxed?" needed the phrase "Social Security income" in the note's lead.
- The student loan interest note gives the 2026 single-filer phase-out ($85,000–$100,000) and points to IRS Publication 970
  for the joint range, because sources disagreed on it.
